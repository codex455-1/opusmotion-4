# Sources and fact notes

## The operation

- **Unit.** ROCAF 35th Squadron — publicly the "Black Cat Squadron" — a
  CIA-supported U-2 unit at Taoyuan, Taiwan. ROCAF history dates the unit's
  establishment to 1 February 1963; training and aircraft delivery ran from
  1959–1961. The US–ROC agreement is named **Project Razor**, with a stated
  period of 13 January 1962 – 24 May 1974.
- **Scale.** About **220 operational missions** from 1961–1974, roughly half
  over mainland China, covering more than 10 million km² and 30 provinces.
- **Losses.** Five U-2s downed by Chinese air defences; four pilots killed and
  two captured, plus training losses. Sources vary slightly (five vs six
  airframes); the film avoids a casualty count on screen.
- **Tabasco.** On **7 May 1967**, pilot **Chuang Jen-liang (莊人亮)** took off
  from a US base in northern Thailand (Takhli), flew over the Lop Nur test
  site, and dropped **two sensors, each about 2 m long**. Six weeks later the
  sensors transmitted signals to Taipei confirming China's first hydrogen-bomb
  test. The target was too far from Taiwan for a round trip from Taoyuan.
  Source: *Taipei Times*, 25 Aug 2010, "Cold War spy pilots lift veil on secret
  missions" (AFP feature).
- **Test 639.** **17 June 1967**, Lop Nur, Xinjiang: China's sixth nuclear test
  and first full-scale two-stage thermonuclear test, air-dropped from an H-6,
  yield **3.3 Mt**, detonation altitude about 3 km. It came 32 months after
  China's first atomic test (Project 596, 16 October 1964, 22 kt).
- **U-2.** Lockheed U-2C, nicknamed "Dragon Lady"; ceiling above **70,000 ft**
  (21,336 m); the aircraft could be tracked by SA-2/HQ-2 batteries and was lost
  to them repeatedly from 1962 onward. The film's altitude figure and the
  "edge of space" line are deliberately poetic, not a claim that 21 km is
  orbital space.
- **End.** Deep-penetration flights were curtailed from March 1968; the
  programme ended in May 1974 and the squadron was disbanded that year, as
  US–China relations changed.

## What is inferred, not documented

- The exact enclosure, power source, antenna and Taipei relay chain of the two
  sensors are not public in the cited reporting. The film shows two sealed
  2 m cases with a whip antenna and a simple pulse, and says only that "it will
  tell Taipei" — matching the reported outcome without asserting hardware
  detail.
- The pilot's pressure-suit portrait is not shown; the film uses a file-card
  typographic treatment instead of a fabricated likeness.
- The 3,000 km figure is the approximate great-circle distance from Takhli to
  Lop Nur. The actual routed distance was longer.
- The film's "six weeks" is the reported interval between the drop and the
  sensor signals, not a precise transmission date.
